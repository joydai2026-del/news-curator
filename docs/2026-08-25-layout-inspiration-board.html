Layout Inspiration Board

2026-08-25 · five directions, each mocked up live with real headlines from your running curator

What you asked for

  • Pictures. Every item gets an image, not a naked line of text.
  • Headline plus a few words. Enough description to know whether to open it, no more.
  • Unfold in place. Click to open the detail inside the feed, close it, keep going. Never lose your position.
  • Scannable on desktop too. The phone feel you like from Perplexity Discover, without missing headlines on a big screen.
  • Clear categories. You can tell at a glance which topic you are in and switch without a page reload.
  • Simple, clean, premium, Apple-minimal. System typography, generous whitespace, hairline borders, restrained colour, light and dark.

Historical recommendation recorded in this board: Direction E

At the time, Direction E was recommended because the live page repeated the same court ruling across six of thirty Claude-topic slots. The proposed hybrid collapsed those outlets into one card, added sticky category chips and a hero card, and used a responsive grid that became one full-width card per row on phones. Direction A was recorded as a smaller alternative without clustering or the hero. This comparison is preserved as design evidence and does not choose the current direction or schedule any implementation.

What the cards need that you do not have yet

Every direction above assumes two fields your curator does not produce today. This is the honest cost of moving from a list to a card feed, and it is worth reading before you pick.

Pictures

Your feeds give you a headline, a link, a source and a time. They do not give you an image. The standard way to get one is to fetch each article page at build time and read its og:image meta tag, the same picture the article would show if you pasted the link into a chat. That works, and it is what most aggregators do, but four things are true about it.

RealityWhat it means for youImpact
One extra HTTP request per articleSixty items a day means sixty fetches on top of the feed pull. Slower builds, and some publishers rate-limit or block a plain scripted fetch the same way Axios and The Verge blocked mine this session.Manageable
Quality varies wildlyReuters, Wired, The Verge and Ars Technica ship good 1200x630 images. Many others ship a stretched logo, a generic stock photo, or the same house image on every article, which looks worse in a card grid than no image at all.Needs a filter
Some sources have none, everarXiv, Hacker News and Show HN posts carry no og:image. On the page I pulled today that is a large share of your AI agents tab, so on a research-heavy day a picture-first grid could be more than half empty.Real problem
Hotlinking versus cachingLinking straight to the publisher's image is free but breaks when they move it, and quietly tells them you are reading. Caching a copy into your repo is more robust and faster, but grows the site and raises a rights question for a public page.Your call

The fallback matters more than the pipeline. When there is no usable image, do not leave a grey rectangle. Render a typographic card: the source name set large in the category colour on a tinted ground, with the topic label. Done well it reads as a deliberate design decision rather than a missing asset, and it is the single thing that decides whether a thin day looks premium or looks broken. That is how the grey blocks in the mockups above should eventually be replaced, and it is worth designing before the scraper is written.

The few words of description

The same gap applies to the two lines of description you asked for, and it is the less obvious of the two. Your curator matches on headlines only, so there is no summary text anywhere in the pipeline. Three ways to fill it, in ascending cost: use the RSS <description> field where the feed supplies one (free, but many feeds leave it empty or stuff it with boilerplate), read og:description from the same page fetch that gets the image (nearly free once you are already fetching, and usually the article's own standfirst), or generate a one-liner with a model at build time (best and most consistent, but it costs money per item per day and introduces the risk of a summary that is subtly wrong on a page with your name on it). The middle option is the one to start with, because it reuses a fetch you are already paying for and the text is written by the publisher rather than invented.

Categories

Your topics.yaml currently defines two topics, AI agents and Claude, both still carrying their example-file comments. Every direction above assumes those become visible category chips. Two things to decide before the redesign, not after: what happens to an item that matches both topics, since the Anthropic hardware story matches each of them today and would otherwise appear twice in an All view, and whether a third topic is coming, because two chips look like an unfinished nav bar while four or five look like a considered one.

The theme, in specifics

All five mockups use the same token set, measured from the real sites rather than guessed. Apple Newsroom's live styles gave the ink at #1D1D1F, the muted grey at #86868B, SF Pro at weight 600 with negative tracking of -0.374px at 17px (about -0.022em), and a plain white ground with no card shadows. Perplexity Discover gave the 3:2 image crop with object-fit cover, which is the ratio the mockups use. The Verge gave the confirmation that a news grid can run at 700-weight headings on a near-black ink and still feel clean, though its custom typeface is doing work a system stack cannot copy. Google News gave the cluster pattern, one topic headline over four outlets with a "see more perspectives" expander, which is the ancestor of the clustering in Direction E.

Concretely, for your stylesheet: ink #1D1D1F on #FFFFFF in light, #F5F5F7 on #000000 in dark, muted #86868B in both, hairline borders at 10 percent black or 13 percent white, 14px card radius, no shadows, one accent colour used only for links, headline tracking around -0.018em, and the system font stack so it renders as SF Pro on your Mac and iPhone with no web font to download. Use the toggle at the top of this page to check both.
